Screen time unlock app: earn the minutes back

Most screen time apps hand you everything, then nag when you have had too much. An unlock app starts from zero and makes you go and get it.

By Shay Gavan · Published 4 August 2026

The short version: a screen time unlock app keeps your apps locked by default. You get minutes back by doing something first. The apps are not a limit you can dismiss, because there is no limit. They are just shut.

What is a screen time unlock app?

A screen time unlock app is an app blocker that starts with your chosen apps locked and gives you access only after you have done something you decided mattered — a workout, a study session, a lesson. It inverts the usual model: a screen time limit gives you everything up front and warns you once you have overspent, while an unlock app gives you nothing up front and hands minutes back as you earn them.

The practical difference is what happens at the moment you want to scroll. A limit is a notification you can dismiss. An unlock is a door that opens when the work is done, so there is nothing to dismiss.

Looking for a particular app by that name? This page is about the category rather than any one product. If you were searching for a specific app, the sections below still cover what that kind of app does, what it can honestly check, and where it is the wrong tool.

Why limits get ignored and unlocks do not

A limit arrives at the worst possible moment. You are already in the feed, already comfortable, and a box appears asking whether you would like to stop. You would not. You tap the box away. Nobody has ever thanked a dialog for interrupting them.

An unlock arrives before any of that. The app is shut when you reach for it, which means the decision happens while you are still capable of making it, not fifteen minutes into a scroll. That is the whole difference, and it is a difference in timing rather than willpower.

This also explains the odd thing people report after switching: they open their phone less without feeling restricted. Nothing is being taken away. It simply was not handed over yet.

How the unlock actually works

You choose which apps are the problem. Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, whichever one you already know the answer to. Those go on the blocked list, and Broc shows your habit list instead of the feed when you open one early.

Then you attach habits, and minutes to each one. Read for twenty minutes, earn fifteen. Train, earn thirty. The numbers are yours. When a habit is done, the minutes land and the apps open.

Two unlock modes exist because two kinds of people exist. Per-habit gives you a chunk of time for each habit as you finish it. All-habits-first keeps everything shut until the whole list is done, which is stricter and better if you negotiate with yourself.

What stops you just lying about it

Honest answer: not much, if you are determined. Broc offers a timer that has to genuinely run, and an AI photo check that looks at what you point the camera at. Both are tripwires for the half-second of self-deception, not vaults. Anyone committed to cheating a habit tracker will cheat a habit tracker.

What they do is remove the easy lie. Tapping a box takes no effort, so people tap it. Sitting through twenty minutes, or pointing a camera at the thing you said you would do, costs enough that the lie stops being free. That turns out to be sufficient most days.

Where an unlock app is the wrong tool

If your problem is one specific app at one specific moment, a pause app like one sec is lighter and might be enough. If you want to see where the hours went before changing anything, the measurement in Digital Wellbeing is free and already installed. Unlock apps suit people who already know which app is the problem, already tried limits, and already watched themselves dismiss them. If that is not you yet, start cheaper.
