An app that blocks social media, and actually holds

You have installed one of these before. It lasted about nine days. Here is why, and what has to be different for the next one to stick.

By Shay Gavan · Published 4 August 2026

Straight answer: any app that blocks social media on Android can be uninstalled by you, in a minute, because it is your phone. The useful question is not how to make a block unbreakable. It is how to make breaking it require more effort than the impulse lasts.

The nine-day pattern

Week one is great. You install the blocker, pick Instagram and TikTok, feel briefly excellent about yourself. Week two you discover the snooze button. Week three the snooze button is muscle memory and the app is decoration. By week four it is uninstalled and you have concluded that blockers do not work.

Blockers work. That specific blocker asked you for permission at the exact moment you were least equipped to refuse. Nine days is roughly how long it takes to learn where the escape hatch is.

Every bypass, and which one is yours

There are only about four, and knowing yours tells you what to pick next.

The editable timer. The block ends at a time you set, and you can change that time from inside the block. This is a suggestion wearing a uniform.

The dismissible pause. A breathing screen, a countdown, a "are you sure". Effective for a week, then your thumb learns the position of the button and stops reading the words.

The quiet uninstall. Nothing can prevent this. What varies is how much friction sits in front of it and whether you have to admit to yourself what you are doing.

The substitute. You block Instagram and open Reddit. The block held perfectly and changed nothing, because the problem was never the specific app. This one needs a different fix and no blocker will get there alone.

What has to change

The negotiation has to leave. Not be made longer or louder, but be absent. If there is a button that ends the block, you will find it on a bad night.

Broc removes it by attaching the unlock to a habit instead of a timer. The apps you picked stay shut, and open when you have done the thing you said mattered. There is nothing to snooze because there is no countdown. The only route through is the one you chose while thinking clearly.

Verification is a timer that has to genuinely run, or an AI photo check of what you point the camera at. Neither is unbeatable, and the honest version of that claim is worth reading. They exist to make the easy lie cost something.

When a lighter tool is the right call

If you have never tried anything, do not start here. ScreenZen is free and its pauses are genuinely enough for a lot of people, and Digital Wellbeing is already on your phone if you only want to see the damage first. Spending money before you know your failure mode is how you end up with four abandoned apps.

Come back to habit-gated blocking when you have watched yourself dismiss a pause and know it is not going to hold.

Setting it up on Android

Pick the apps that pull you in. Add one habit you would do anyway. Set what it is worth in minutes. Broc needs Usage Access to see which app is open and Display Over Apps to show your habits instead of the feed, both granted during setup and covered in the full tour.

Block one app first, not all of them. The people who block everything on day one are the same people who uninstall it on day four.

The feed opens when the habit is done. No snooze, because no timer.
