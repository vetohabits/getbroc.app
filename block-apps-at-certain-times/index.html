How to block apps at certain times on Android

Scheduling is the right answer for some problems and quietly the wrong one for others. Here is how to set it up, and how to tell which you have.

By Shay Gavan · Published 18 August 2026

Straight answer: Android does this without any extra app. Settings → Digital Wellbeing & parental controls → Focus mode, pick the apps, set the schedule. Whether that solves your problem is a separate question, and worth two minutes before you set it up.

The built-in way

Android’s Digital Wellbeing includes Focus mode, which pauses a chosen set of apps on a schedule you define. It is free, it is already on the phone, and for a genuinely time-shaped problem it is usually enough. You can also take a break from it on demand, which is either the feature or the flaw depending on you.

Third-party blockers offer the same scheduling with more control: multiple schedules, stricter modes that are harder to switch off, and blocking of websites as well as apps. If the built-in version keeps getting turned off in the moment, that extra resistance is what you are paying for.

When a schedule is the right tool

Schedules work when the boundary really is a time. Lectures from nine to eleven. No phone after eleven at night. Work hours where you are at a desk anyway. In each case you are not trying to make yourself do something — you are trying to keep the phone out of a block of time that already has a shape.

When a schedule quietly fails

The trouble starts when people reach for a schedule to solve a task problem. You want to study, so you block social media from seven to nine. Two things then happen. The first is that you can sit through seven to nine without studying, because the block has no idea what you are doing, only what time it is. The second is that at nine o’clock everything opens whether or not the work happened, which teaches you that waiting is enough.

If you have ever set a schedule and then simply used the time to reorganise your desk, that is the failure mode. The window was never the problem.

Using both at once

These are not rivals and there is no reason to pick one. A schedule handles the hours where the phone should simply be absent, and a gate handles the work that keeps slipping. A common setup is a hard schedule from bedtime until morning, because sleep is genuinely time-shaped, with a habit gate running during the day for the two or three apps that eat the afternoon.

The one thing worth avoiding is stacking several blockers over the same apps. Two blockers fighting over the same package tend to produce confusing behaviour, and the usual outcome is that you disable both.

The gate alternative

A habit gate ignores the clock. The apps are locked, and they open when the habit is done — at six in the morning or eleven at night, it makes no difference. It suits the task-shaped problem for the same reason a schedule suits the time-shaped one: the condition matches the thing you actually want.

To be clear about what Broc is: Broc is a habit gate and does not block on a schedule. If what you need is a nine-to-five window, Android Focus mode or a scheduling blocker will serve you better and cost nothing. Broc is for the case where the block should lift because you did the thing, not because a clock moved.
